# Pilot 001 — frozen deterministic result

Status: synthetic validation result; frozen summary.

Research direction, conceptual framing and methodology: Linda Thorstensen.

## Question tested

Can the Mimulus measurement scaffold distinguish source-supported persistence from decoder dependence, unsupported structure, targeted ablation effects and a controlled history intervention on known-answer material?

This is a test of the measurement machinery. It is not a test of the Voynich Manuscript and is not empirical evidence for a general theory of observers or memory.

## Frozen fixture

Fixture: `synthetic-decoder-persistence-001` v1.0.

The unaltered source is an A–B–A block structure with known boundaries at zero-based indices 4 and 8. Two perturbations are declared in advance:

- `permuted`: preserves symbol counts while destroying the A–B–A order relation; its known A–B–A answer key is empty.
- `ablated`: removes the high middle-block contrast while preserving repeated outer-block position, so different decoder families should react differently.

## Decoder families

The comparison uses three materially different declared families, blinded in the exported record:

- `Q7`: local adjacent-change threshold;
- `L4`: repeated prefix/suffix motif recurrence;
- `N9`: history-adaptive adjacent-change threshold, reported by consensus across two observers.

These codes are masking devices, not semantic categories.

## Result

The scaffold behaves as intended on the frozen fixture.

1. On the original source with reset or identical history, all three decoder families recover `[4, 8]`. The meta-observer labels both boundaries `SOURCE_SUPPORTED_INVARIANT` because agreement coincides with the independent known answer.
2. With trained or swapped heterogeneous histories, the history-adaptive consensus no longer recovers those boundaries while the two non-history families do. The same source features are therefore labeled `DECODER_DEPENDENT_SUPPORTED`, not erased from the answer key.
3. After count-preserving order permutation, local-change decoders still propose boundaries even though the A–B–A answer key is empty. Those candidates are labeled `DECODER_DEPENDENT_UNSUPPORTED`; coherence or agreement is not promoted to source truth.
4. Under targeted contrast ablation, the local-delta and history-adaptive families lose the boundaries while motif recurrence retains `[4, 8]`. This demonstrates that a source perturbation can selectively remove the evidence used by one decoder without destroying a different preserved relation.
5. The designed history-control pattern passes on the original source and disappears under the contrast ablation, so the scaffold does not interpret history divergence as a free-standing invariant.

All 12 deterministic cells are recorded in `frozen-summary.json` and are generated by `pilot/engine.js`. `pilot/test.js` asserts the key behaviors.

## What this establishes

Pilot 001 establishes only that the current implementation can represent and keep separate:

- independent source truth;
- cross-decoder agreement;
- decoder-specific support;
- unsupported decoder output;
- targeted source sensitivity;
- controlled history dependence.

That is sufficient to use the same record architecture for a historical pilot without changing the meaning of these distinctions after seeing historical outputs.

## What it does not establish

It does not show that cross-decoder persistence is generally informative outside the fixture. It does not validate any linguistic decoder. It does not establish a memory mechanism in an LLM. It does not support a Voynich translation or decipherment claim. It does not make these experimental labels canonical ArcTopia terminology.
